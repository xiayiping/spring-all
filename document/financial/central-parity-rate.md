# central parity rate


The **central parity rate** is the official "midpoint" exchange rate that the People's Bank of China (PBOC) sets each business day for the Chinese yuan (CNY). It serves as the benchmark reference point around which the yuan's actual trading value is allowed to fluctuate.

Here's how it works:

### ⚙️ How It's Set
The China Foreign Exchange Trade System (CFETS), authorized by the PBOC, publishes this rate at **9:15 AM Beijing time** each business day.

The calculation method differs by currency:
*   **Against the US Dollar (USD/CNY)** : This is determined by taking a **weighted average** of prices offered by major market makers (large banks) before the market opens. The highest and lowest offers are excluded, and the rest are averaged based on each bank's trading volume and quoting performance.
*   **Against Other Major Currencies**: For currencies like the Euro, Yen, and British Pound, the central parity is either calculated using the USD/CNY rate as a cross-reference (e.g., with Hong Kong Dollar and Canadian Dollar) or derived from a simple average of market maker quotes for direct trading.

### ⚖️ Why It Matters
The central parity rate acts as an **anchor for daily trading**. On the interbank spot market, the yuan's actual trading price is only allowed to move within a specific "band" around this midpoint:
*   Against the **US dollar**, the trading price can fluctuate by up to **2%** above or below the central parity.
*   Against other major currencies like the **Euro and Yen**, the allowable band is wider, at **3%**.

In simple terms, the central parity rate is the official "starting price" for the yuan each day. It provides stability and transparency by clearly defining the benchmark from which all daily market movements are measured.
